The Watson Lake Sign Post Forest began in 1942 during the construction of the Alaska Highway. A U.S. Army engineer, Private Carl K. Lindley, was recovering from an injury and assigned to repair a damaged mileage signpost near Watson Lake. Feeling homesick, he added his own sign pointing to his hometown of Danville, Illinois, 2,835 miles away. Other soldiers followed his example, and after the war, travelers continued adding signs. What started as one homesick soldier’s marker has grown into a forest of tens of thousands of signs from all over the world.
