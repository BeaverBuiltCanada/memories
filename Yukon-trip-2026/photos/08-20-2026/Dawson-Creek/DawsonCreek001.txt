The Alaska Highway was built in 1942 as a wartime emergency project after the attack on Pearl Harbor. The United States needed a land route to supply and defend Alaska, especially after Japanese forces moved into the Aleutian Islands. Construction officially began in March 1942, and more than 11,000 U.S. Army engineers and thousands of civilian workers carved a 1,500‑mile road through mountains, muskeg, and permafrost in just eight months. The highway begins at Mile 0 in Dawson Creek, British Columbia, which became the official starting point of the route.
