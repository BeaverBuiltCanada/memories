<h5>Watch Your Step…</h5>
the dune habitat is home to some rare plants and insects.

Ten or more species of insects found in the Carcross Dunes are found in few other places on earth.

Scientists studying the dunes have found eight species of insects that may be new to science.

<h5>The Coast Dart Moth</h5>
This moth is found in the dunes of Europe, Asia, Carcross and Whitehorse (Alaska). These moths are carnivorous that feed on the roots of a variety of dune plants.
(Photo: Garth Pohl)

There are only six Baikal Sedge (Carex sabulosa) populations in Yukon, one in Alaska, and another in Asia. This species has been listed as a threatened under the federal Species at Risk Act.  
(Photo: FRC photo)

<h5>The Dune Tachinid Fly</h5>
(Germaria angustata) and other rare insects can be found at the edge of the Carcross Desert.
In July you might see a female Dune Tachinid Fly looking for a place to lay her eggs. She will lay them on the wing of a fly larva hatch and attack a host caterpillar, perhaps a larva of the Coast Dart Moth. The Dune Tachinid Fly is difficult to distinguish from other blackish flies that hover over the dunes.
(Photo: FRC photo)

<h5>The Yukon Lupine</h5>
(Lupinus kuschei) is a rare species found in the dunes. This plant is endemic to the Yukon, meaning it is found nowhere else in the world. It can also be found along the Aishihik Road.
(Photo: Plants by Paul Catling)
