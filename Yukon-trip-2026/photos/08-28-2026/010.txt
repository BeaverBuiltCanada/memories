<h5>Growing on Shifting Sands</h5>
The plant communities that live on the dunes are constantly changing as the dunes move and alter their shape. Established plant communities are disturbed and new species invade all dunes.
Plants that live on the dunes have to survive droughts, the abrasive effects of wind‑borne sand and unstable ground. The plants of the Carcross Dunes have adapted to these extreme conditions.

<h5>Unstable Dunes</h5>
The most active and fragile dunes, characterized by little or no vegetation, are found on the higher elevations and near the shore of Lake Bennett.
Here you will find plants that can live in harsh and unstable conditions. For example, the roots of the Ball sage and Siberian sand sedge are long and fibrous, and help anchor the plant and stabilize the sand. (PC House)

<h5>Semi‑stable Dunes</h5>
On less‑exposed dunes, shrubs and trees develop a mat‑like growth form in response to the abrasive action of the wind.
In winter and spring, accumulated snow and ice protect the bottom leaves and branches while exposed parts of the plant are blasted by wind‑driven ice and sand.
Mistle growth struggles to keep life and stable. (PC House)

<h5>Stable Dunes</h5>
In sheltered areas, more complex mixed shrub surroundings are found. The roots of willow shrubs are strong and help stabilize the dunes.
Even wind and rain can disturb the stability of the dunes and cause them to begin again.
A dune modeling is stable front. (PC House)

<h5>WALK SOFTLY</h5>
The Carcross Dunes are an exciting place to explore but this fragile habitat needs our help. Please avoid disturbing dunes and slopes where vegetation is sparse.
Limit motorized vehicles to areas where there is no established trail.
