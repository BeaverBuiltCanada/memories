<h5>CHANGING CLIMATE</h5>

<h5>Turning Tundra to Forests</h5>
As the climate warms, trees and shrubs are moving northward into areas that were once tundra. This changes habitat for animals that depend on open spaces.

<h5>Newcomers</h5>
Warmer temperatures allow new species to move into the Yukon. Some thrive, while others compete with long‑established plants and animals.

<h5>Tinderbox Forest</h5>
Hotter, drier summers increase the risk of forest fires. Large fires can reshape the landscape for decades.

<h5>Running out of Refugia</h5>
Some species rely on cool, stable environments to survive. As these areas shrink, plants and animals may struggle to find suitable habitat.

<h5>Winter Survival</h5>
Shorter, warmer winters affect animals that depend on deep snow or long cold periods. Changes in snowpack and freeze‑thaw cycles can make survival more difficult.
