<h5>Shekūłeh | At een | Harvesting</h5>

The two traditional languages of the Carcross/Tagish people are Tagish and Tlingit. The Tagish name for Caribou Mountain, which you see in the distance, is Medziheh Shechē — “wind blowing on the forehead.” The Tlingit name for the mountain is Dax̱a̱t Kʼeidi — “wind blowing against the face.”

The frequent wind — along with a near lack of rain and the effects of ice‑age glaciers — has created the Carcross Desert. The prevailing winds constantly blow sand over the area, shaping dunes of sand. Despite these harsh conditions, rare species of plants, including Yukon lupine and Baikal sedge, grow here.

For the Carcross/Tagish people, harvesting plants, lichen, bark and berries for food and medicine has always been part of the seasonal round. Knowledge of harvesting has been passed down over the years from Elders to a younger generation. The Carcross/Tagish people continue to have a profound connection with the resources provided by the land and the water.
