<h5>Artful Chemistry</h5>

Emerald Lake’s brilliant blue‑green colour comes from sunlight reflecting off a white layer of “marl” on the lake bed. Marl is a calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) clay that forms when dissolved carbon dioxide (CO₂) reacts with calcium (Ca) in the water.

Limestone hills around the lake were created 150–200 million years ago by corals and marine organisms. About 14,000 years ago, glaciers carved out the valley, leaving behind the basin that became Emerald Lake.
