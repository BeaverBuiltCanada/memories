Over 46 years, Dredge No. 4 recovered eight metric tons of gold.

Lost digging time meant lost money, as the dredge shut down only briefly for crews to clear out the sluices. The gold‑laden dirt was then taken back to Bear Creek Camp for final sorting and melting into bricks.

At top production, almost 22 kg (50 lbs/800 oz) of gold were cleaned out every three to four days. Twice a month the company would ship a dozen new gold bricks to the bank.

In the Gold Room at the Bear Creek Camp, the final gold was formed into bricks valued at more than $15,000 each. From there, the bricks were shipped to the Bank of Canada in Ottawa for minting into coinage.

Dredge No. 4 recovered eight metric tons of gold from Bonanza Creek and the Klondike River between 1913 and 1959.
