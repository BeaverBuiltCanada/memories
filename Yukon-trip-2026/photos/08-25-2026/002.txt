<h5>FIVE FINGER RAPID</h5>

Five Finger Rapid is a section of the Yukon River where swift water and narrow channels posed a serious hazard to riverboats during the Klondike Gold Rush. Many sternwheelers struggled to navigate the tight passage, and accidents were common. Eventually, the underwater obstacle that created the worst of the rapids was blasted away, making the route safer for river travel. Today, the distinctive rock formations remain, and the river still squeezes between the “fingers,” offering a glimpse into the challenges faced by early travellers on the Yukon River.
