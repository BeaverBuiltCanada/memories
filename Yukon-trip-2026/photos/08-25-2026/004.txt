<h5>THE KLONDIKE STAMPEDE</h5>

During the 1898 Klondike Gold Rush, thousands of prospectors navigated the hazardous 800‑kilometre (500 mile) route from Bennett Lake to Dawson City. Five Finger Rapids was a major obstacle along the Lake and more than a few stampeders ended up in the water after choosing the wrong channel!

“…a great barrier looms up a mile ahead – five great irregular blocks of reddish rock ranging across the river like the piers of a bridge – no perceivable channel. That on the left is growing ominously over shallow rocks, so we turn to the right and dash into a small cove a few hundred feet from the great wall. We climb up and look at the rapid. It seems ugly and dangerous. The opening is about one hundred feet wide, with wet, sheer walls, through which the river drops a couple of feet, the waves rising angrily in a foaming turmoil, and the rapidly diminishing drops until lost in the swift current below. We turn our prow squarely for the middle of the cleft: a drop, a smash, a few quarts of water over the sides, and we are shot through into the fast current, without even looking back.”

— Harper Albury, The Klondike Stampede, Vancouver: UBC Press.
