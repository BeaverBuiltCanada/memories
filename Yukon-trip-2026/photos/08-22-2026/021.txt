Kaska Dena  
Kaska traditional territory spans a vast region across Yukon, British Columbia, and the Northwest Territories. Their land use extended north to the Pelly River watershed, east to the Mackenzie Mountains, and south to the Liard River watershed. Traditional camps were found throughout this territory, including the Rancheria River, Frances River, Finlayson Lake, and the Yusezy River.

The Kaska maintain strong cultural traditions such as storytelling, drumming, tanning, and beadwork, and their communities continue to live throughout their traditional lands. In Yukon, Kaska Dena live in Watson Lake, Upper Liard, and Lower Post. They are represented by the Liard First Nation in Watson Lake and the Ross River Dena Council in Ross River. In British Columbia, the Kaska Dena Council represents communities including Lower Post and the Kwadacha Nation at Fort Ware.

Kaska Language  
Kaska is one of eight Indigenous languages spoken in Yukon. An Athapaskan language, it is closely related to Tahltan in British Columbia and Tagish in Yukon. Cultural pride is reflected in the use of Kaska place names, and revitalization efforts continue to strengthen the language for future generations.
