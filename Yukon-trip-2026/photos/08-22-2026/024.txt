Trading Posts  
At the turn of the 19th century, Kaska people maintained wide trade networks, exchanging goods with inland groups and with European traders through Tlingit and Tahltan intermediaries. These intermediaries connected them to the Hudson’s Bay Company on the Pacific coast and to the Russian American Company before Alaska was sold to the United States in 1867.

The discovery of gold on the Stikine River in 1861, and later in the Cassiar region, brought prospectors and traders into the area. Rugged terrain and dangerous rivers made access difficult, and many trading posts opened and closed quickly, sometimes changing names, owners, or locations.

With the rise of the fur trade, land‑use patterns shifted as the Kaska focused more on trapping fur‑bearing animals. People typically visited trading posts once a year to exchange goods and share news. These posts created new social connections with outside traders but also disrupted long‑standing Indigenous trade relationships.
