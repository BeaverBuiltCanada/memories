Life on the Land  
For thousands of years, people have lived throughout this region, moving seasonally in small family groups and relying on detailed knowledge of the land. The Kaska followed a yearly cycle of hunting, fishing, trapping, and berry picking, traveling between traditional camps and resource-rich areas.

Fish Lake and Fishing Lake have long been important for their abundance of fish, and Key Point was a major salmon harvesting site. People continue to gather resources today—berries, fish, birds, mammals, firewood, and timber—while trappers and residents hunt and trap for both food and fur. Local fish include lake trout, Arctic grayling, and northern pike. Large mammals include sheep, goat, caribou, moose, and bear, while beaver, marten, and lynx thrive in the boreal forests.

Cold winters bring opportunities for skiing, snowshoeing, dog‑sledding, and snowmachine travel. Summer offers long sunny days, wildflowers, and countless ways to explore the land through boating, fishing, hiking, and other outdoor pursuits.
