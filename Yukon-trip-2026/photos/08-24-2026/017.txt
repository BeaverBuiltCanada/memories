<h5>A Triassic tropical paradise</h5>

<h5>A time of rebirth</h5>
The Triassic period (251–200 million years ago) marked the first major diversification event on Earth following the mass extinction that ended the previous Permian period. Life rebounded and flourished in the seas and on land.

<h5>Triassic in the Yukon</h5>
The oldest rocks exposed in the Whitehorse area date from the Triassic period, around 225 million years ago. At that time, the southern part of the Yukon was located near the equator, in a warm, tropical ocean. Pacific coral reefs and volcanic islands formed off the coast. The rocks built up in those reefs and islands are now part of the Whitehorse area’s mountains. The east side of Whitehorse, including Grey Mountain and Mount Sima, is part of this ancient coral reef system.
