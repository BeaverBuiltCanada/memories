<h5>A Jurassic landscape</h5>
<h5>The birth of a mountain chain</h5>
The beginning of the Jurassic period (200 million years ago) marked the start of the breakup of the supercontinent Pangea. Magma leaked along the rift and filled the gaps between the separating land masses with new oceanic crust.
As North America drifted westward, the Pacific plate slid beneath it along the subduction zone. The result was a chain of volcanoes that spewed lava and ash. The heat and pressure from the volcanic activity metamorphosed the sediments of the continental shelf into new rocks such as schist and quartzite. Conglomerates, sandstones and shale also formed from erosion of the volcanic rock into the adjacent ocean basin.

Jurassic flora and fauna  
Globally, the climate was warm and humid, resulting in plentiful plant and animal life. The basin included primitive fish, ammonites, and early marine reptiles. On land, large conifers and giant horsetail-like ferns dominated the landscape.
