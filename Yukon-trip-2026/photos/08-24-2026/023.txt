<h5>Sam McGee's original cabin, built in 1899.</h5>
<h5>He was a southerner. What was Sam McGee doing up north before the Gold Rush?</h5>
Sam McGee wasn’t a prospector at first. He was a Southerner from Tennessee, and he originally came north as a railway worker. Before the Klondike Gold Rush exploded, he was employed on the Yukon narrow‑gauge railway projects, helping with construction and maintenance during the early development of transportation routes in the territory.

When the Gold Rush hit, he — like thousands of others — shifted into prospecting and odd jobs around Whitehorse. His cabin (built in 1899) comes from that period of early settlement and work, not from the poem itself.
