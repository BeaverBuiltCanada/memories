<h5>Whitehorse Copper Belt</h5>

Copper skarn formation: Gold wasn’t the only element drawing prospectors to the Yukon. In the early 1900s, copper was mined in the Whitehorse area. The Whitehorse Copper Belt runs from the city through the surrounding hills.
The 30 copper deposits that make up this belt is a type of metamorphic rock formation known as skarn. Skarns form when magma intrudes carbonate rock, which includes limestone. The magma heats the limestone and chemically reacts with it. The resulting rock is rich in minerals that contain metals like copper. Cooling magma and the chemical reactions with limestone created the rich deposit of copper-bearing skarn belt we see today.
