<h5>A View into the Ancient Past</h5>

<h5>Canyon Mountain (Grey Mountain)</h5>
The grey limestone rocks of Canyon Mountain formed about 220 million years ago as coral reefs in an ancient sea. The rocks underlying this part of Yukon originated in a much warmer climate than today. Since that Triassic era, the Canyon Mountain limestone has been folded, compressed, and moved to the north.

<h5>Mount Lorne</h5>
The rocks under Mount Lorne are volcanic, deposited layer upon layer as the result of explosive eruptions throughout the Cretaceous period, about 100 million years ago.

<h5>Golden Horn</h5>
Golden Horn may look like a volcano but it is mainly formed of folded sedimentary rocks like sandstone, laid down 180 million years ago during the Jurassic period. The Miles Canyon basaltic lavas likely erupted from a small vent at the base of Golden Horn only 8.5 million years ago.

<h5>Glaciation</h5>
Ice covered this area during the most recent period of glaciation, about 20,000 years ago. The flowing ice sheet carved the mountains into their distinctive rounded shapes. As the ice sheet melted, about 15,000 years ago, thick deposits of silt, sand, and gravel were left in the valley bottom, covering the bedrock, and forming the rolling hills that surround Whitehorse.

<h5>Miles Canyon</h5>
The rocks of Miles Canyon are among the youngest in southern Yukon. Overlapping flows of basalt lava cooled in the Yukon River valley about 8.5 million years ago. As the lava cooled, it created five or six-sided columns that cracked. The river rushed over the basalt and worked away at the cracks to erode the rock and form the canyon.

<h5>Whitehorse Copper Belt</h5>
About 112 million years ago, magma from deep in the earth’s crust rose and pushed through cracks in the existing limestone layer to form rich copper deposits. The copper was first discovered by prospectors in 1898 and periodically mined until 1982, contributing significantly to the territorial economy.
