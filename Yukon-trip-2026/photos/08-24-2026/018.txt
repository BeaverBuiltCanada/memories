<h5>A Journey into the Recent Past</h5>

Miles Canyon and White Horse Rapids  
were once the most dangerous obstacles along the 3,200-km length of the Yukon River. A dam, built in 1959, harnessed the rapids and raised the water levels in the canyon, eliminating a huge eddy.

During the 1898 Klondike Gold Rush, crudely built boats and inexperienced navigators caused many accidents and more than a few deaths here. Norman Macaulay rose to the challenge and quickly built a tramway along the east bank of the river. His company charged 3¢ per pound and $25 per boat to draw freight-laden carts along peeled-log rails. The tracks extended for five miles from Miles Canyon to a point just above present-day Whitehorse.

After Macaulay was established, John Hepburn built a competing tramway along the route of an old portage trail on the opposite riverbank. When the rush of stampeders ebbed, Macaulay bought out Hepburn for a reported $60,000 and operated both tramways for a short time. In 1899, the tramlines were sold to the White Pass & Yukon Route, the company constructing a railway from Skagway to Whitehorse.

Look Closely  
The rail beds of both the Macaulay and Hepburn Tramways can still be seen along the banks of the Yukon River.
